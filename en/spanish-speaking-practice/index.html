SPANISH SPEAKING PRACTICE

How to Start Speaking Spanish When You're Still a Beginner

Maybe you studied Spanish in school. You learned basic vocabulary, practiced verb conjugations, completed worksheets, and perhaps even passed a few exams.

But when it comes to actually speaking Spanish with another person, you freeze.

Or maybe you're a complete beginner. You've only just started learning Spanish and think: "I'm not good enough to have a conversation yet."

If that sounds familiar, you're not alone.

Speaking is a different skill from studying a language — and you don't have to wait until you're "ready" to start practicing it.

"I studied Spanish in school, but I can't speak it"

This is a very common experience.

You might recognize words like hola, gracias, ¿cómo estás?, and remember some grammar from school. But knowing Spanish and producing Spanish in real time are two different things.

In a real conversation, there is no worksheet in front of you. Someone asks you a question, and suddenly you need to understand it, find the words you know, build a sentence and actually say it.

If you haven't practiced doing that, even very basic Spanish can feel surprisingly difficult.

The good news is that you don't need to start over. You may simply need more opportunities to turn the Spanish you already know into spoken Spanish.

Can complete beginners practice speaking Spanish?

Yes.

You don't need to wait until you've studied Spanish for six months or completed a certain level before you start speaking.

A beginner conversation can be extremely simple.

Hola.
Me llamo Sarah.
Soy de Estados Unidos.
Me gusta viajar.
Estoy aprendiendo español.

That's already Spanish.

Your first goal doesn't need to be having a deep 30-minute conversation. It can simply be getting comfortable saying a few sentences to another person.

Start with five minutes instead of a full lesson

A long conversation can feel intimidating when you're a beginner. Five minutes is different.

In five minutes, you could practice:

  • introducing yourself
  • saying where you're from
  • talking about your hobbies
  • ordering food
  • describing your day
  • using five new words you've recently learned

Then you're done. You don't need to fill an entire hour with Spanish. And the next time, you can try again.

Short conversations make it easier to focus on one small thing at a time — especially when you're still building confidence.

You don't need to prepare for every conversation

Another barrier to speaking practice is scheduling.

Traditional online lessons often mean choosing a teacher, booking a time and preparing yourself for a longer session. That works well for many learners.

But sometimes you simply want to practice the Spanish you already studied.

That's why I created QuickLesson.

QuickLesson is built around short, 5-minute online conversations. Instead of committing to a long lesson, you can join during an available session and practice speaking Spanish with an instructor who is online.

You might only manage a few sentences. That's completely fine.

The purpose of those five minutes is not to prove how much Spanish you know. It's to use it.

From "I know some Spanish" to "I can speak some Spanish"

I created QuickLesson after years of learning languages myself. I started learning Spanish at 19 and tried many different ways of studying and practicing languages online.

One thing became very clear to me: studying a language and actually speaking it are not the same thing.

You don't have to stop using textbooks, apps, classes or videos. Instead, speaking practice can be the place where you take everything you've learned elsewhere and finally try to use it with another person.

And if you're a complete beginner, you don't have to wait until some imaginary day when your Spanish is "good enough."

Start with what you know. Even if that's only:

Hola, me llamo…

Five minutes is enough for a first conversation.
